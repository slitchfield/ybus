# Where the power flows spend their time, 2026-11-04

Phase O2's profile: where `powerflow`'s power flows spend their time, measured with a sampling
profiler, to decide what to accelerate first. Seven workloads on four cases where time matters,
each sample attributed to a stage of the computation.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Code | The `dev/pf-profile` branch, at the commit that adds this record |
| Machine and system | As in the earlier records: Intel Core i7-14700K (28 hardware threads), Ubuntu 24.04.3 under WSL2, Rust 1.97.1 |
| Build | `--profile profiling`: the release profile with debug line tables, so the code is the same optimized code the surveys time |
| Profiler | samply 0.13.1, sampling at 1 kHz (software CPU clock, since WSL2 has no hardware counters), with `perf_event_paranoid` at 1 |
| Run | `scripts/profile-pf.sh`: the `profile_pf` example runs each workload's whole pipeline (parse, model, solve) repeatedly for 5 s, at least once, so each stage weighs as in one run; `scripts/profile_summary.py` folds the samples into stages |

A sample's stage is the innermost frame on its stack that belongs to one: for example, an
allocation inside the Jacobian refill counts as allocation, and the refill's own arithmetic as
assembly. A parallel run's shares are of the samples on all its threads (CPU time), and its
"threads" column is those samples over the run's wall time. Some libc routines have no symbols
(they appear as `fun_…`); they're counted with their caller's stage, which understates
allocation and copying where they are memset or memcpy.

Per-run times agree with the earlier records where they overlap: for example, one-at-a-time
reactive limits on case_ACTIVSg70k take 601 s per run here (with parsing and the model) against
577.5 s for the solve alone in [`ac-reactive-limits-reuse-2026-11-03.md`](ac-reactive-limits-reuse-2026-11-03.md).

The workloads: `dc` is the DC power flow, `nr`, `fdxb` and `fdbx` the AC power flow by Newton's
method and fast-decoupled XB and BX, `qlim2` Newton's method with reactive limits enforced one
at a time, and `n1` and `n1-parallel` every single-branch outage by rank-k updates of B′'s
factorization, serially and with rayon.

## What the profile shows

- **N-1 outages spend little time on the sparse solves.** The triangular solves the method
  exists for take 7–9% on the three larger cases (`Lu::solve_sparse`; on case2383wp the study's
  one factorization is the larger part, 6%); the rest goes into building each outage's full
  result: `DcStudy::outage`'s dense update of the angles, flows and outputs (27–47%), and
  allocating, zeroing and copying those vectors (35–55%, `Vec::from_elem`, `malloc`, growth),
  with unnamed memset and memcpy routines under `outage` besides. In parallel, allocation's share
  rises (44–55%) and 20–27 of the 28 threads are busy on average.
- **One-at-a-time reactive limits are refactorization.** `Lu::refactor` takes 41–64% (most on
  the largest cases, which take 10,993 and 14,225 Newton iterations). On case_ACTIVSg70k, the
  Jacobian refill (`source_value`) takes 9% and sorting inside `AcModel::solve_round` 6%.
- **A single Newton solve** on a large case is mostly factorization: refactorization 25% and the
  first, pivoting factorization (`gilbert_peierls`) 19%, then parsing 15%, AMD ordering 8%,
  assembly 11% and allocation 11–12%.
- **A single DC or fast-decoupled solve** is dominated by everything but the solve: parsing the
  case file takes 25–48%, AMD ordering 9–21% and allocation 11–20%; the factorizations take
  8–13%.

## Decision: what to accelerate first

Each target keeps results bit-identical, as decided for phase O2; the bound is the speedup if
the stage cost nothing (Amdahl's law), not a promise.

1. **N-1 outages without per-outage allocation.** Reuse the result buffers across outages (or
   let a caller receive each outage's results in place), so an outage writes into memory it
   already has. Allocation and copying are 35–55% of the N-1 time, more with the unnamed memset
   and memcpy, so removing them bounds the gain at 1.5–2.2×, serial and parallel; in parallel it
   should also relieve the allocator contention.
2. **The LU refactorization kernel.** `Lu::refactor` is 41–64% of one-at-a-time reactive limits
   and 25% of a large Newton solve. Faster refactoring in the same operation order (memory
   layout, fewer indirections, unchecked inner loops where the pattern guarantees bounds) keeps
   results bit-identical. Halving its cost bounds the gain at 1.3–1.5× for reactive limits.
3. **Parsing.** 25–48% of a single DC or fast-decoupled run and 15–21% of a single Newton run
   (line handling, `strip_line`, and float parsing). It matters for one-shot runs, not repeated
   solves of one case, so it comes after the two above; parsing must still give the same
   doubles.

Smaller follow-ups the profile also points to: the sorting in `AcModel::solve_round` (about 6% of
one-at-a-time reactive limits on case_ACTIVSg70k), allocation inside the Newton loop (11–17%), and
reusing AMD's ordering where the pattern is known (9–21% of single DC and fast-decoupled
solves). The CUDA spike follows the CPU work, with Nsight Systems.
