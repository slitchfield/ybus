# COLAMD against AMD on power-flow matrices, 2026-11-03

The survey for `Ordering::Colamd`: on all 78 MATPOWER 8.1 cases, the AC Newton Jacobian (at a
flat start) and the DC B′ (Bbus over the PV and PQ buses), each factored by LU under AMD (on
`A + Aᵀ`, keeping large enough diagonal pivots) and under COLAMD (on `A`'s columns, with plain
partial pivoting). The survey's output, one row per case, is in
[`colamd-2026-11-03.csv`](colamd-2026-11-03.csv).

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Code | The `dev/colamd` branch, at the commit that adds this record |
| Machine and system | As in the earlier records: Intel Core i7-14700K, Ubuntu 24.04.3 under WSL2, Rust 1.97.1, release profile |
| Run | `scripts/benchmark-ordering.sh`, from 07:17 to 07:19 EST, with the default limits |

Fill counts L's and U's stored entries, the diagonal once. Each analysis and factorization is
timed together by its median over repeated runs, for up to 1 s or 101 runs. A third fill column
factors under COLAMD's ordering but with AMD's diagonal preference
(`analyze_lu_with_permutation`), separating the ordering from the pivoting.

## Results

| | Jacobian | B′ |
|---|--:|--:|
| Cases where COLAMD fills less than AMD | 0 of 78 | 0 of 78 |
| COLAMD's fill over AMD's, median (range) | 1.45× (1.00–1.85×) | 1.26× (1.00–1.79×) |
| The same, on the 27 cases of 1,000 buses or more | 1.67× | 1.51× |
| With diagonal pivots preferred under COLAMD's ordering, median | 1.36× | 1.16× |
| COLAMD's time over AMD's, median (from 1,000 buses) | 1.93× (2.27×) | 1.90× (2.16×) |

- **AMD stays the default.** Both matrices are structurally symmetric with strong diagonals, so
  AMD on `A + Aᵀ` with diagonal pivots is the better bet on every case, as the Phase 3 decision
  expected. COLAMD never fills less, and ties only on two 4-bus cases (case4_dist, case4gs).
- **The ordering, not the pivoting, makes the difference.** Preferring diagonal pivots under
  COLAMD's ordering recovers a little (1.36× against 1.45× on the Jacobian), but most of the gap
  remains. COLAMD orders for `AᵀA`, whose factor bounds the fill under *any* row pivoting: a
  guarantee that costs fill when the diagonal pivots would have done.
- **Where COLAMD is for:** a pattern far from symmetric, or one whose diagonal can't be pivoted
  on. Its own tests show that case: on structurally unsymmetric matrices with their rows
  shuffled, COLAMD's LU fills 8.7% less than AMD's, and it orders `AᵀA` within 0.3% of AMD on
  the explicitly formed `AᵀA` (`sparse_mat/tests/colamd_test.rs`).
