# The KKT matrix factored by a pivoted LDLᵀ, 2026-11-08

This is phase O4's second target, chosen from [`ipm-profile-2026-11-07.md`](ipm-profile-2026-11-07.md):
a symmetric indefinite factorization of the KKT matrix, in place of its LU.

- **The factorization** (#57) is `sparse_mat`'s multifrontal LDLᵀ with threshold Bunch–Kaufman
  pivoting.
- **The solvers' option** (#58) is `KktFactorization::Ldlt`, with the predictor-corrector method's
  inertia correction.
- **This survey** runs every MATPOWER case with costs through both, decides the defaults, and
  closes the target.

Static pivots were tried first and set aside (#56). In the solvers they failed where LU
converges.

## The method

- **The factorization** (#57): a multifrontal LDLᵀ on the Cholesky analysis's elimination tree
  (AMD), with fundamental supernodes and threshold Bunch–Kaufman 1×1 and 2×2 pivots in each
  dense front. A pivot that fails the test is delayed to the parent front.
- **No regularization:** a 2×2 pivot handles a zero diagonal.
- **The inertia** comes from D's blocks.
- **In the solvers** (#58, `interior_point::kkt`):
  - the KKT matrix is assembled exactly symmetric;
  - the analysis is kept while the pattern holds;
  - the pivot threshold is u = 1e-4;
  - each solve is refined normwise, for up to 3 steps.
- **The inertia correction** (predictor-corrector method only): when the inertia isn't
  (nx, neq), W is shifted by δ_w I and refactored. This is Wächter and Biegler's Algorithm IC,
  2006, §3.1, with their defaults.

- **Large AC cases gain most:** 2.4–3.0× on the predictor-corrector method's largest. Most of
  their time is the factorization, which LDLᵀ cuts about 5× (#57).
- **Small cases lose, most on DC:**
  - **On DC below 1,000 buses,** LDLᵀ is 0.47× (MIPS) and 0.55× (the predictor-corrector
    method) in the median. The slowest is case69 at 0.34×.
  - **Why:** the multifrontal factorization has a fixed cost per front. It allocates each dense
    front, and its kernel swaps rows and rescans its candidates. The DC OPF's small and very
    sparse KKT matrices have many tiny fronts, where that cost outweighs the smaller factor.
- **Delays make two DC outliers:**
  - **case3375wp** on the predictor-corrector method is 0.15×, with 8.2× LU's peak memory
    (123 MiB against 15).
  - **case13659pegase** on MIPS is 0.4×, with 3× the memory. Delayed pivots enlarge the fronts
    above them.

## The inertia correction

| | Cases with corrections | What changed |
|---|--:|---|
| DC, predictor-corrector | 1 (case8387pegase, 1 correction) | nothing |
| AC, predictor-corrector | 16 | case8387pegase lost its convergence, case9241pegase took 20 more iterations, two cases took fewer |

- **Where it fired:** 16 AC cases, from case9 (3 corrections) to case13659pegase (214). The
  others are case9Q, case145, case300, case1951rte, case2848rte, case2868rte, case6468rte,
  case6470rte, case6495rte, case6515rte, case8387pegase, case9241pegase, case_ACTIVSg10k and
  case_SyntheticUSA.
- **No outcome improved,** and no case newly met the bar.
- **Without it,** every outcome and every iteration count is LU's.
- **The cost:** total time is 2.70× faster without it, against 2.61× with it.
- **Where it does help** is the small nonconvex problems in the tests: on a saddle QP it reaches
  the minimum where LU converges to the saddle.
- **Why it can't help more here:** without a line search it can't do more on the OPF. It turns
  the step towards descent, but the method has no merit function to judge it.

## The defaults

**The rule, decided before the survey:**

- **MIPS keeps LU,** for MATPOWER parity, bit for bit.
- **The predictor-corrector method switches to LDLᵀ** only where two things hold. Every case
  where LU meets the bar, LDLᵀ meets it too. And it's faster in the median.

**Applied per model, as the OPF's options are** (decided with the maintainer, 2026-11-08):

- **AC: switched.** `AcOpfOptions::mehrotra()` now factors by LDLᵀ, without the inertia
  correction. It loses the bar nowhere, and it's 1.12× faster in the median and 2.70× in total.
- **DC: not switched.** `DcOpfOptions::mehrotra()` keeps LU. LDLᵀ loses the bar nowhere and is
  2.05× faster in total, but its median is 0.79×, from the small cases. It qualifies once the
  kernel's cost per front comes down.
- **`MehrotraOptions::default()`** keeps LU.
- **The inertia correction is now off by default** (`inertia_correction: false`), as the survey
  favoured, and stays available as an option.

**Checked:** after the change, a snapshot of every solver's DC and AC OPF solutions on the 72
normalized cases up to 3 MB hashes as before. That covers MIPS, DC and AC, and the predictor-
corrector method on DC. Only the AC predictor-corrector solutions differ, as intended.

## What's left

- **The kernel's cost per front,** which loses the small cases: no allocation per pivot, a
  blocked update, and relaxed amalgamation of DC's tiny fronts.
- **The structural delays:**
  - **where they come from:** zero-diagonal constraint rows that AMD makes leaves of the tree,
    with no partner for a 2×2 pivot, 8,400 to 48,000 on the large cases (#57);
  - **what would remove them:** a KKT-aware ordering, the checklist's study of the KKT
    ordering;
  - **what they cost now:** the delay outliers above.
- **A globalization** (a merit function or filter line search) for the predictor-corrector
  method. The inertia correction would need one to pay off.
