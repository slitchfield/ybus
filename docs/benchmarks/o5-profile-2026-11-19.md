# Phase O5's profile and targets, 2026-11-19

This is phase O5's first record, as `ipm-profile-2026-11-07.md` was O4's. O5 gathers O2's
untried CPU ideas (parallelism within one solve, SIMD, layouts, the triangular solves) and
O4's "Parallel and GPU" item. This record re-times the OPF workloads, profiles them, and
measures how much parallelism each one offers. It then sets the four targets' bounds,
decided with the maintainer (2026-11-19):
1. batched OPF solves
2. parallelism within one solve
3. SIMD and layouts
4. a GPU KKT factorization

**Every profile runs 1.0 threads busy** on a machine with 28. Today rayon runs only DC outages
and sensitivities, behind the optional `rayon` feature.

**The targets' bounds, in short:**
- **Batched solves:** about ×10 on 14 threads, for any solver. It's the only target that
  helps MIPS much.
- **Within one solve:** at most ×1.5–2.1 (DC) and ×2.6–3.0 (AC) for the predictor-corrector
  method. For MIPS it's at most ×1.12, since its LU factorization (77–91% of its time) isn't
  tree-parallel.
- **SIMD in the dense kernel:** at most ×1.14–1.23, if the kernel isn't vectorized already.

The default order stands.

## How it was measured

- **The machine:** an Intel Core i7-14700K (8 performance cores with two threads each, and 12
  efficiency cores: 28 threads) under WSL2, Linux 6.6. The release build ran one workload at a
  time.
- **The code:** `dev/o5-profile` at `02cd373`, whose cold path is `main`'s bit for bit (the
  72-case snapshot is unchanged).
- **The re-timing:** `scripts/benchmark-opf.sh` (a median over repeated runs, at least one) for
  case2383wp, case3120sp, case9241pegase, case_ACTIVSg10k and case_ACTIVSg25k. It ran DC and
  AC, MIPS on LU, and the predictor-corrector method at its defaults: LDLᵀ without the inertia
  correction, the matched ordering on DC and AMD on AC (`ldlt-uncorrected`).
- **The profiles:** `scripts/profile-pf.sh`'s samply profiles of the `dcopf`, `dcopf-mehrotra`,
  `acopf` and `acopf-mehrotra` workloads, on case2383wp, case9241pegase and case_ACTIVSg25k,
  folded into stages by `scripts/profile_summary.py`.
- **The parallelism each solve offers:** the `o5_parallelism` example, from each solve's own
  LDLᵀ analysis (`KktStatistics::tree`, a `FrontTree`). The front tree's work over its critical
  path bounds what parallelism over independent subtrees can give, for the factorization and
  for one triangular sweep. Its rows are in
  [`o5-profile-2026-11-19-parallelism.csv`](o5-profile-2026-11-19-parallelism.csv).
- **The machine's ceiling for independent work:** the parallel DC N-1
  (`YBUS_BENCH_PARALLEL=1 scripts/benchmark-n1.sh`) at 1, 2, 4, 8, 14 and 28 threads
  (`RAYON_NUM_THREADS`).

## The parallelism one solve offers

The predictor-corrector method's KKT front trees, at its defaults:

| Case | Model | Fronts | Height | Factorization bound | Sweep bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| case118 | DC / AC | 64 / 123 | 6 / 7 | ×2.5 / ×3.9 | ×2.9 / ×4.4 |
| case2383wp | DC / AC | 883 / 1,890 | 11 / 13 | ×5.9 / ×4.0 | ×10.8 / ×10.7 |
| case3120sp | DC / AC | 1,074 / 2,592 | 11 / 18 | ×5.1 / ×3.6 | ×11.0 / ×9.1 |
| case9241pegase | DC / AC | 3,606 / 6,945 | 20 / 27 | ×8.9 / ×5.9 | ×19.7 / ×15.7 |
| case_ACTIVSg10k | DC / AC | 3,879 / 6,405 | 20 / 24 | ×6.8 / ×4.4 | ×18.2 / ×13.3 |
| case_ACTIVSg25k | DC / AC | 8,616 / 14,630 | 24 / 27 | ×7.0 / ×4.9 | ×20.0 / ×16.0 |

- **Each tree has one root,** and the root fronts are heavy: amalgamation merges small fronts
  into larger ones, and the separators near the root are dense. That's why the factorization's
  bound grows slowly, ×4–9 on the large cases.
- **A sweep parallelizes better** (×9–20), since its work per front is linear in the front's
  rows rather than quadratic.
- **AC's bound is below DC's** on every large case. Its trees are deeper and heavier near the
  root, likely from the Hessian's coupling of each bus's angle and magnitude. That's an
  inference, not measured here.

## The machine's ceiling for independent work

The parallel DC N-1, which takes each outage on its own, sets how far this machine scales
work with no shared state:

| Case | 2 threads | 4 | 8 | 14 | 28 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| case9241pegase (2.98 s serially) | ×1.88 | ×3.44 | ×5.57 | ×10.0 | ×11.2 |
| case_ACTIVSg25k (11.1 s) | ×1.92 | ×3.88 | ×8.43 | ×10.1 | ×8.68 |
| case_ACTIVSg70k (89.7 s) | ×1.77 | ×3.12 | ×4.41 | ×4.99 | ×4.91 |

- **Scaling stops at about ×10–11 by 14 threads,** and the efficiency cores add little beyond.
- **case_ACTIVSg70k stops at ×5,** consistent with memory bandwidth: its outages touch more
  data than the caches hold.

So **×10 is the ceiling** this record assumes for any parallel stage, unless its own bound is
lower.

## The targets' bounds

### 1. Batched OPF solves

- **What it is:** many independent solves sharing a base, each on its own thread. For example,
  N-1 OPF, warm from the base solution with PR D's `map_start`, or the hours of a day.
- **Bound:** the machine's ceiling, about ×10 on 14 threads for cases up to case_ACTIVSg25k's
  size, for both solvers. MIPS gains as much as the predictor-corrector method, since nothing
  inside a solve needs to split.
- **What there is to gain,** from #87's outage survey: 100 outages of case9241pegase's AC take
  726 s cold on MIPS and 376 s warm at 1e-6 serially. On the predictor-corrector method, they
  take 174 s cold and 100 s warm. At ×10, those are about 38 s and 10 s.
- **Bit for bit:** each solve runs serially on its own data, so a batch at any thread count is
  bit for bit the serial loop's.

### 2. Within one solve

These are the profiles' shares, each parallel stage divided by the smaller of its tree bound
and the ceiling (Amdahl's law):

| Workload | LDLᵀ factorization (share ÷ bound) | Evaluation (÷10) | Triangular solves (÷ bound) | Bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DC, predictor-corrector, case2383wp | 42% ÷ 5.9 | — | 13% ÷ 10 | ×1.86 |
| DC, predictor-corrector, case9241pegase | 32% ÷ 8.9 | — | 8% ÷ 10 | ×1.54 |
| DC, predictor-corrector, case_ACTIVSg25k | 40% ÷ 7.0 | — | 19% ÷ 10 | ×2.08 |
| AC, predictor-corrector, case2383wp | 35% ÷ 4.0 | 31% | 8% ÷ 10 | ×2.62 |
| AC, predictor-corrector, case9241pegase | 35% ÷ 5.9 | 26% | 12% ÷ 10 | ×2.70 |
| AC, predictor-corrector, case_ACTIVSg25k | 37% ÷ 4.9 | 27% | 15% ÷ 10 | ×3.04 |

- **The LDLᵀ factorization** counts the dense kernel, front assembly and the remaining numeric
  factorization.
- **MIPS:** its LU's numeric factorization (77–91%) is a left-looking kernel, not a front tree,
  so only its evaluation splits. That's at most ×1.12 on AC (case2383wp) and ×1.00 on DC.
  Parallelizing the LU is outside these targets, and MIPS's default stays bit for bit.
- **Bit for bit:** the subtrees factor independently, and each child's update must be
  extended into its parent in the serial order. The evaluation's elements must be split by fixed
  ranges, never by thread count.

### 3. SIMD and layouts

- **The dense kernel** (`bunch_kaufman::factor_front`) takes 16–25% of the predictor-corrector
  method's time. Four lanes of f64 (AVX2) on its rank-k update would make it at most ×1.14–1.23
  faster per solve. That assumes the kernel isn't already auto-vectorized, which the target's
  PR checks first in the compiled code.
- **The triangular solves** take 8–19%, and a supernodal or blocked sweep is the layout half of
  this target.
- **Allocation and copying** takes 5–12%, which the layouts target could reduce.
- **Bit for bit:** an elementwise update vectorizes without reordering any element's
  operations, and Rust doesn't contract to FMA unasked. A vectorized reduction reorders its
  sums, so it would be opt-in under O3's bar.

### 4. A GPU KKT factorization

- **What it is:** a cuDSS spike for the KKT factorization, behind `cuda`. It comes last, and
  only if the KKT factorization is still the largest stage of a large AC solve after targets 1–3.
- **What O2 measured:** in its CUDA spike, transfers and host work bounded whole-result
  workloads, and N-1 screening won only from about 25,000 buses. A GPU factorization needs
  matrices large enough to amortize its transfers.

## The order

The bounds keep the default order: batched solves, then within one solve, then SIMD and
layouts, then the GPU.
- **Batched solves** have the largest bound (×10), help both solvers, and are bit for bit by
  construction.
- **Within one solve** comes next (×1.5–3.0, the predictor-corrector method only).
- **SIMD** bounds lowest (×1.14–1.23) and touches the most numerically sensitive code.
- **The GPU** is gated on the others.
